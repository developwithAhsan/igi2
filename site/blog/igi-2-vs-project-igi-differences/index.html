IGI 2 vs Project IGI: What Changed in the Sequel?

Comparing Project I.G.I. (2000) with IGI 2: Covert Strike (2003): story, missions, publishers and what the sequel built on.

By Ahsan · November 21, 2026 · 3 min read

Project I.G.I.: I’m Going In arrived in 2000 and introduced David Jones, a former British SAS operative turned agent. Three years later, IGI 2: Covert Strike took the formula and expanded it. Here is how the two games compare.

The basics

Project I.G.I. (2000): developed by Innerloop Studios and published by Eidos Interactive.

IGI 2: Covert Strike (2003): developed by Innerloop Studios and published by Codemasters.

Missions: 14 in the first game and 19 in the sequel.

The story

The original sends Jones to Tallinn, Estonia, to extract a kidnapped arms dealer, Josef Priboi, who holds vital information about a stolen US W-88 nuclear warhead. His Pentagon handler is Rebecca Anya. IGI 2 brings back familiar names, Anya and Priboi, but raises the stakes: stolen EMP chips, a rogue Chinese general named Wu Xing and a rocket launch.

What stays the same

Both games are about patience: scouting, planning and choosing between stealth and firepower. The hero is the same, and both keep the objective-driven mission structure that lets you choose how to finish a task.

Which should you play first?

IGI 2 stands on its own, because each briefing tells you what you need. If you enjoy backstory, the first game explains why names like Priboi and Anya matter. See how it all connects in our IGI 2 story explainer.
